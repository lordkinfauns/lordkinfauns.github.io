Lord Kinfauns

Timeline

The lordship of Kinfauns runs as a single thread from medieval Scotland to the west of Ireland. The timeline below follows that line — from its legendary beginnings with the pirate-knight Sir Thomas Charteris, through the Charteris lords and the title's merger into the Earldom of Moray, to its present home at Newhall in County Clare.


The Pirate
"Red Reaver"
Sir Thomas Charteris

According to legend the first lord of Kinfauns (lost charters): Sir Thomas de Longueville, later named Charteris. He was a native of France, and representative of a family well known in that country; but at the close of the thirteenth century, when at the court of Philip the Fair, having a dispute with one of the noblemen, he slew him in the king’s presence. Being refused pardon for the rash and bloody act, he betook himself to the high seas, and under the name of the Red Reaver, became terrible to all seafaring people as a remorseless pirate.

Sir William Wallace, however, in his voyage to France, having met and encountered this formidable giaour, took him prisoner, and pleased with that kindred spirit of heroism, which would have immortalised the reaver in a better cause, presented him to the French king as a suppliant for mercy. Philip, who was pleased to have an occasion of obliging the Scottish hero, and of exhibiting a signal instance of his royal clemency, cancelled the sentence of outlawry, and conferred on his penitent subject the honour of knighthood.

Thus restored to court favour, and to society, Charteris accompanied Wallace to Scotland, where he ever after remained his steady friend, and a frank participator in all his daring exploits. When Wallace was betrayed and carried into England, and Bruce asserted his right to the Scottish crown, Charteris was the first that followed him into the water at the taking of Perth, in January 1313, and in reward for his bravery, received a royal grant of estate and title Lord of Kinfauns.

Sir William Charteris

Succeeded his father as Lord of Kinfauns and had a charter of confirmation (now lost) from James Douglas, son and heir of Sir William, Earl of Douglas, for those of Lumphanan in 1378, which had previously been granted by John Randolph, Earl of Moray, to Sir Patrick de Carnoto, and confirmed by Robert Stewart, Earl of Fife, 1391.

Sir Thomas Charteris of Cagnore

Had a charter (now lost) for the half lands of Kinfauns as heir to his father on 5 April 1408 and had a licence from Robert de Ross of Craigie allowing him to take water through his lands on 10 July 1412, in which that Robert styles him his “beloved brother.”

He resigned his lands of Grubet, Yhethame, Corbet, Holden and Turifmuirside, all in the Roxburghshire, to Nicholas de Rutherford on 12 May 1426 and died after then and before October 1443.

Sir William Charteris of Cagnore

Succeeded and was served heir to his grandfather, Thomas Charteris, in the lands of Lumphanan on 1 October 1443. He married firstly, to Christian, daughter of Sir Robert, 1st Lord Erskine, (she was widow of Patrick, 1st Lord Graham, who had died in 1465 and by whom she had issue), with whom he is named in a complaint on 9 July 1476;

and secondly, by 13 July 1470, to Agnes Cochrane, when her liferent was confirmed to her in a charter (now lost) by her husband. Surviving that wife, he married thirdly, to Elizabeth Stewart, who survived him and is styled as his relict on 31 October 1479. He died after 3 February 1479 and before the 31st October following.

de jure

1st Lord Kinfauns
Sir Thomas Charteris

The title was granted by King James III of Scots, on 13 July 1487, the first recorded dominus Kynfawnis Lord Kinfauns in the Great Seal in crown charter (earlier charters possibly lost), in favour of whom his father conveyed the lands of Haltoun, Craigton, Pitmorche, Craigour all in the lordship of Lumphanan, and those of Kinfauns and Pitsindy, in the lordship of Kinfauns.

He married by 5 November 1471, to Euphemia Bruce when his father took instruments warning them to surrender the lands of Craigquarter and Little Cagnor in implementation of the terms of a letter of reversion and so that he may infeft them in the £20 lands of Cagnor. This was not complied with and on the 19th November following Sir William took back the lands by the act of “taking of earth and stone from the ground there.” He died before October of 1520.

2nd Lord Kinfauns
Sir John Charteris

Styled son and heir to Sir Thomas Charteris of Kinfauns when witness to a charter by William Comyn of Inverallochy of a by which donated his lands of Audiall to the University of Aberdeen on 17 September 1506 and was served heir to his father in the lands of Kinfauns on 2 October 1520.

He married by July of 1519, to Euphemia, daughter of David Lindsay, 8th Earl of Crawford, when they had a charter from his father for the lands of Haltoun and Craigton of Lumphanan, and with whom he had a charter of confirmation under the Great Seal for those of Golkton, Ribno and Tulyhow, in Perthshire, and the lands of Little Cagnore, in Stirlingshire, on 17 March 1525. He died after then and by 18 August 1534.

His son Thomas Charteris, was outlawed therefore did not have his title recognised by the King, he was infeft in the lands of Cagnoir as heir to his deceased father on 18 August 1534 which he had apprised from him on 15 May 1545, for having murdered James Ross and ruined the mill of Craigton belonging to that James and Marjorie Stewart, his spouse.

He was served heir to his grandfather, Thomas Charteris, in the lands of Lumphanans on 17 April 1546. He married firstly, to Janet Stewart from whom he separated, and secondly, to Katherine, sister of John Graham, 3rd Earl of Montrose, (she was widow of Sir James Chisholm, 2nd of Cromlix, by whom she had issue. She married for a third time, following Thomas Charteris’s death, to Archibald Ogilvy of Lawton, and died on 25 December 1583), who is styled his relict in an action she raised against Janet Stewart, the first wife of her late husband, on 20 March 1556.

He was killed in 1552 by James Ross son and heir of the deceased James Ross of Craigton and his kinsmen and friends in revenge for his father’s death and was succeeded by his brother, John.

3rd Lord Kinfauns
Sir John Charteris

John succeeded his brother on his untimely death in 1552, to whom he was served heir on 10 April 1553, and on 11 March 1553 he and his brother’s widow, Katherine Graham, raised an action against those persons responsible for his brother’s murder.

He married by 17 May 1551, to Janet, daughter of Sir James Chisholm, 2nd of Cromlix, when they disponed their lands of Lumphannan to Mr Patrick Carnegy, and, following the death of his only surviving child, his son George, he adopted Henry Lindsay as his heir who is afterwards styled Henry Lindsay alias Charteris filius adoptive John Charteris of Kinfauns. He died about 1600 and was father of George Charteris, Master of Kinfauns.

GEORGE CHARTERIS, MASTER OF KINFAUNS, was the only son and married Katherine, daughter of Alexander Blair, fiar of Balthayock. On 7 November 1575, he was witness with his father to a charter by Patrick Cheyne of Essilmont granting the lands of Essilmont to Helen Bruce, relict of George Hay, Earl of Errol, and his future wife, and by 17 January 1578.

4th Lord Kinfauns
13th Earl of Crawford

Henry Linsday later became Harry Charteris via adoption, later Lord of Kinfauns until 1608 when he gifted the lordship with lands as a wedding present to his son, he is known as Lord of Carelstoun in the charter, later inheriting the earldom of Crawford but only enjoying the earldom for the last two years of his life.

5th Lord Kinfauns
Sir John Lindsay

Son inherited Lordship of Kinfauns as a wedding present but later tragically predeceased father. In his capacity as King James I of Great Britain, France and Ireland - the King confirmed the title for the wedding in a royal charter, under the Great Seal, in 1608.

Earls of Kinnoull

6th Lord Kinfauns
1st Earl of Kinnoull
Sir George Hay

Sir George Hay, born in 1570, rose to prominence as a distinguished statesman and landowner.

In 1621, he succeeded to lands and lordship, as 6th Lord Kinfauns, a title tied to the barony, before being elevated to the Viscountcy of Dupplin in 1627 and further to the Earl of Kinnoull in 1633 by King Charles I. His career included appointments as Lord Clerk Register, Privy Councillor, and Lord Chancellor of Scotland, reflecting his political acumen.

Hay married Margaret Halyburton, with whom he had three children. His eldest son, George Hay, inherited his titles and lands upon his death in 1634.

7th Lord Kinfauns
2nd Earl of Kinnoull
Sir George Hay

Sir George Hay, born in 1596, was the eldest and only surviving son of Sir George Hay, 1st Earl of Kinnoull, and Margaret Halyburton. Between 1621 and 1634, he was styled Lord Kinfauns, a courtesy title derived from his father’s title as the 6th Lord Kinfauns, before succeeding as the 2nd Earl of Kinnoull and 7th Lord Kinfauns in 1634.

George Hay distinguished himself as a military commander during his early life, serving on the continent in various campaigns. His military role is documented in Dutch historical records, where he is referenced as Baron of Kinfauns, reflecting his courtesy title. His experience and connections further bolstered the Hay family’s prominence in both Scotland and abroad.

In 1641, a ratification by Parliament reaffirmed his infeftment of the Lordship of Kinfauns, including its extensive lands, hereditary rights, and responsibilities, such as the keeping of the waters of the Tay. This legal confirmation solidified his authority over the barony and ensured its continued integration into the family’s estate.

Hay also served as a yeoman of the guard to King Charles I and was appointed to the King’s Privy Council, a reflection of his influence and loyalty to the Crown during a turbulent period in British history. His role in these positions underscored his importance both politically and socially.

Sir George Hay died on 5 October 1644.

Blair and Carnegie Lordship

8th Lord Kinfauns
Sir Alexander Blair of Balthayock

Sir Alexander, born in 1631, played a pivotal role in the transfer and consolidation of the barony of Kinfauns, a significant estate in Perthshire. In 1647, Sir Alexander succeeded to the lordship of Kinfauns. In February 1654, he and his wife, Dame Elizabeth Fotheringham, formally granted the lands and lordship of Kinfauns, along with associated rights and properties, to their son, William Blair of Kinfauns through a Crown-backed disposition. This included hereditary rights such as the office of Baillie of the Tay, teind sheaves, and salmon fishings on the River Tay.

The transaction was ratified by the Scottish Parliament, ensuring its legal validity and providing guarantees against any future challenges. Sir Alexander further secured the deal by offering his own lands of Balthayock, Nether Balmyles, and Potento as collateral, underlining his commitment to the agreement.

Knighted around 1650, Sir Alexander held significant roles in local governance, serving as a Commissioner for the Sheriffdom of Perth in 1661 and as a Justice of the Peace in 1663. In 1687, he was made a burgess of Arbroath, further solidifying his regional influence. Sir Alexander died in 1692.

9th Lord Kinfauns
Sir William Blair of Kinfauns, 1st Baronet

Sir William Blair of Kinfauns was created the 1st Baronet of Kinfauns on 18 September 1666 in the Baronetage of Nova Scotia. A prominent Scottish nobleman, he held the lordship of Kinfauns and played a significant role in maintaining the family’s estates and legacy. His baronetcy was granted with a remainder to heirs male of his body, but Sir William had no sons, only three daughters.

Following his death, the baronetcy became extinct, and the lordship of Kinfauns passed to his eldest daughter, Ann Blair. In 1673, Ann and her husband, Hon. Alexander Carnegie, secured a Crown charter formally recognising their rights to the lordship, ensuring the continuation of the estate under new stewardship. Sir William's death marked the end of the Blair baronetcy but not the enduring influence of his lineage.

10th Baroness of Kinfauns
Ann Blair

Ann Blair was the daughter of Sir William Blair of Kinfauns. In 1671, she married Hon. Alexander Carnegie, son of David Carnegie, 2nd Earl of Northesk. Through a financial arrangement with her father, Ann and Alexander received a crown charter for the lands and barony of Kinfauns on 28 February 1673. This charter established Ann as the 10th Baroness of Kinfauns. The couple had at least one son, Alexander Blair Carnegie, who succeeded as the 11th Lord Kinfauns in 1695.

11th Lord Kinfauns
Alexander Blair Carnegie

Born around 1672, he was the son of Alexander Carnegie and Anna Blair. Through his mother, he inherited the Kinfauns estate, adopting the combined surname Blair Carnegie. In 1695, he succeeded to the lands and lordship of Kinfauns.

Lords of Gray

12th Lord Kinfauns
John 11th Lord Gray

He married, 17th October 1741, Margaret Blair, heiress of Kinfauns in Perthshire, by which marriage the Lordship of Kinfauns and property came into possession of the family. He had four sons, three of whom, seceded to the title, and seven daughters.

13th Lord Kinfauns
Charles 12th Lord Gray

Born to John 11th Lord Gray, and Margaret Blair, Charles succeeded his father in 1782. His tenure as Lord was relatively short, lasting only four years until his death in 1786.

14th Lord Kinfauns
William 13th Lord Gray

Younger brother to Charles, William ssumed the title upon Charles's death. His period as Lord Gray spanned two decades, during which he managed the family estates.

15th Lord Kinfauns
14th Lord Gray
Sir Francis Gray

The youngest son of the 11th Lord Gray, Francis assumed the title in 1807. He had a distinguished public service career, notably serving as a Scottish representative peer from 1812 to 1842, representing Scotland in the House of Lords. Additionally, he held the position of Postmaster-General of Scotland, reflecting his involvement in national affairs. Francis was instrumental in the construction of the new Kinfauns Castle in the 1820s, showcasing his commitment to enhancing the family estate.

16th Lord Kinfauns
John 15th Lord Gray

Son of Francis Gray, John continued the family's tradition of public service. He served as a Scottish representative peer from 1842 to 1867, actively participating in legislative matters. John was also a Deputy Lieutenant of Perthshire, indicating his significant role in local governance. His tenure saw the further development of the Kinfauns estate, maintaining its prominence in the region.

17th Baroness of Kinfauns
Madeline 16th Lady Gray

Sister to John, Madelina inherited the title upon his death. She remained unmarried throughout her life, and her tenure as Baroness of Gray lasted until her death in 1869.

18th Baroness of Kinfauns
17th Lady Gray
Margaret Murray

Niece of Madeline. As read on her tombstone, her ladyship was 18th in succession to the barony (lordship of Kinfauns), which now merges into the Earldom of Moray while the lordship of Gray separated in another line.

Earls of Moray

19th Lord Kinfauns
15th Earl of Moray
Edmund Arhibald Stuart Gray

Cousin from 11th Lord Gray. He married Anna Mary Collinson on 6 September 1877. In 1878 he succeeded to the Lordship of Kinfauns, and in 1895, to the Earldom of Moray. During his tenure, he managed the family estates and upheld the responsibilities of the earldom. His lordship died on 11 June 1901, leaving no direct heirs, and was succeeded by his younger brother, Francis James Stuart.

20th Lord Kinfauns
16th Earl of Moray
Lieut. Col Francis James Stuart

Lieutenant Colonel Francis James Stuart, 16th Earl of Moray, was a distinguished Scottish nobleman and military officer. He pursued a military career, achieving the rank of Lieutenant Colonel in the Liverpool Regiment.

On 24 June 1879, he married Gertrude Floyer Smith, daughter of Reverend Francis Smith and Mary Isabella Bogue. The union, however, did not produce any offspring.

In 1901, upon the death of his elder brother, Edmund Archibald Stuart, 15th Earl of Moray, Francis inherited the earldom, becoming the 16th Earl of Moray. His tenure as Earl was marked by his commitment to public service; he held the positions of Justice of the Peace for Dorset, Fife, and Perthshire, and served as Deputy Lieutenant of Perthshire. These roles underscored his dedication to civic responsibilities and the well-being of his communities.

Francis James Stuart died on 20 November 1909 at the age of 66, leaving no direct heirs. Consequently, the earldom was succeeded by his younger brother, Morton Gray Stuart, who became the 17th Earl of Moray.

21st Lord Kinfauns
17th Earl of Moray
Morton Gray Stuart

Born on 16 April 1855 in Blandford, Dorset, he was the youngest son of Reverend Edmund Luttrell Stuart and Elizabeth Jackson. Educated at Cambridge, he earned a Master of Arts degree, preparing him for a life of leadership and service.

In 1890, he married Edith Douglas Palmer, daughter of Rear-Admiral George Palmer. The couple had four children, including two future Earls of Moray. Morton succeeded his brother, Francis James Stuart, as the 17th Earl of Moray in 1909, taking on the stewardship of the family estates, including Darnaway Castle and Kinfauns Castle.

A passionate horticulturist, Morton greatly enhanced the gardens at Kinfauns and was known for his commitment to public service, serving as a Justice of the Peace and Deputy Lieutenant of Perthshire. He died on 19 April 1930, leaving a legacy of dedication to his family and estates, succeeded by his son, Francis Douglas Stuart, as the 18th Earl.

Morton succeeded his elder brother, Francis James Stuart, as the 17th Earl of Moray in 1909. His tenure as Earl was marked by a focus on estate management and public service. Known for his passion for horticulture, he significantly enhanced the gardens at the family’s estates, including the famed Darnaway Castle and Kinfauns Castle. His efforts not only preserved but also enriched the cultural and environmental legacy of these historic properties.

In addition to his responsibilities as Earl, Morton served as a Justice of the Peace in several counties, including Dorset, Fife, and Perthshire. He also held the position of Deputy Lieutenant of Perthshire, reflecting his active involvement in local governance and civic duties.

Morton Gray Stuart died on 19 April 1930 at Darnaway Castle, leaving behind a legacy of dedication to his family and the Moray estates. His eldest son, Francis Douglas Stuart, succeeded him as the 18th Earl of Moray, continuing the lineage of this distinguished family.

22nd Lord Kinfauns
18th Earl of Moray
Francis Douglas Stuart

Francis Douglas Stuart was born on 10 July 1892, the eldest son of Morton Gray Stuart, 17th Earl of Moray, and Edith Douglas Palmer. He served as a Captain in the Scottish Horse and the Royal Air Force during World War I, earning the Military Cross for his bravery.

On 21 June 1924, he married Barbara Murray in New York, USA. They had three daughters: Lady Mary Anne Stuart, Lady Sarah Gray Stuart, and Lady Arabella Stuart.

Succeeding his father in 1930, Francis became the 18th Earl of Moray. He was appointed Lord-Lieutenant of Morayshire on 2 August 1935, serving until his death on 9 July 1943, a day before his 51st birthday.

23rd Lord Kinfauns
19th Earl of Moray
Archibald John Morton

He was born on 14 November 1894 in Edinburgh, second son of the 17th Earl of Moray, Morton Gray Stuart, and Edith Douglas Palmer. Educated at the Royal Naval Colleges, he served in the Royal Navy during both World Wars, reaching the rank of Lieutenant-Commander.

In 1922, he married Mabel Helen Maud Wilson, with whom he had four children, including his successor, Douglas John Moray Stuart. Archibald inherited the earldom in 1943 following the death of his elder brother, Francis.

He served as Deputy Lieutenant of Morayshire and remained dedicated to public and military service throughout his life. His lordship died on 27 March 1974 in Forres, Scotland, at the age of 79.

24th Lord Kinfauns
20th Earl of Moray
Douglas John Stuart

Born on 13 February 1928 in Johannesburg, South Africa, his lordship grew up on a cattle ranch in Botswana before returning to Scotland in 1943. Educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, and the Royal Agricultural College, he became a skilled estate manager and led the Moray Estates Development Company, driving significant diversification and modernisation.

In 1964, he married Lady Malvina Murray, with whom he had two children, including John Douglas Stuart, the 21st Earl of Moray. A lifelong car enthusiast, Douglas founded the Doune Motor Museum and the Doune Hill Climb, solidifying his passion for motorsport.

His lordship also contributed to heritage preservation, placing Doune Castle into the care of Historic Scotland in 1984. He died on 23 September 2011, leaving a legacy of leadership, cultural preservation, and innovation.

25th Lord Kinfauns
21st Earl of Moray
John Douglas Stuart

John Douglas Stuart, son of Douglas John Moray Stuart, 20th Earl of Moray, and Lady Malvina Murray. Educated at Ampleforth College and St. Andrews University, his lordship carries forward the family’s distinguished legacy.

As the 21st Earl, he has overseen the Moray Estates Development Company, continuing the work of modernising and diversifying the family's extensive landholdings. His leadership emphasises sustainable development and community engagement, ensuring the estate’s ongoing relevance in contemporary Scotland.

His lordship also maintains the family’s commitment to heritage, presiding over historic properties such as Darnaway Castle. His tenure reflects a balance of tradition and innovation, ensuring the Moray lineage remains integral to Scottish nobility.

Commane Lordship

26th Lord Kinfauns Chief of Ó Comáin

Fergus Lord Kinfauns, Gaelic Clan Chief of Ó Comáin, a descendant of Irish Kings and successor of a medieval Irish chiefdom, Clan Commane was recognised by Clans of Ireland, under the patronage of Michael D. Higgins, President of Ireland. He married Maria Lady Kinfauns (née Nadolski), with whom he had three children. Through marriage, he is connected to the ancient Polish noble house of Warzęta Korwin z Ślepowrony, part of the Polish Szlachta (nobility) since 1224.

A Roman Catholic and native of County Clare, Fergus grew up as one of 11 children on his parent’s farm, land which was once part of Newhall, a historic Irish gentry estate. A few miles from the chiefdom's ancestral capital Cahercommane. Like many young Irish people of his generation, he moved to London to seek new opportunities. He became hugely successful in business. Deeply connected to his roots, Fergus harboured a lifelong dream of restoring Newhall to its former glory. After years of unsuccessful attempts to purchase the estate, fate intervened, and the property became available, allowing him to fulfil that dream.

Together with his wife, Maria Lady Kinfauns, and the Commane family, Newhall has been restored into a cherished family home and a wellness retreat that celebrates its folklore, spiritual history, and cultural significance. Their efforts breathe new life into national treasures such as Killone Abbey and the Holy Well of St John, ensuring Newhall remains a vibrant part of Ireland's heritage.

Fergus succeeded to 26th Lord Kinfauns (but does not use the title), a title previoulsy tied to the prestigious Earldom of Moray, one of the original seven earldoms of Scotland. Read more about the title and Newhall here.

In recent years, Fergus contributed in the recognition of Clan Commane Ó Comáin by Clans of Ireland, based on DNA matching and academic research into the Chiefdom of Tulach Commáin, of County Clare in the Gaelic nobility of Ireland. Following this, Fergus was consecrated as Taoiseach, by derbhfine under Brehon law, reflecting the traditional Gaelic-Irish method of clan chief succession.
