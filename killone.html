Killone Abbey: A Hidden Gem in Co Clare

Tucked away in a quiet valley along the shores of Killone Lake, Killone Abbey is among County Clare’s most evocative medieval sites. Built in the late 12th century by Donal Mór O’Brien, King of Thomond (the name for Clare before the 16th century), this Augustinian convent offers a rare glimpse into medieval Ireland.

You’ll find Killone Abbey in the ‘Barony of Islands,’ about three kilometres southwest of Clarecastle village and four kilometres south of Ennis. Its location is stunning, right by the northern shoreline of Killone Lake (or Lough), with a nearby ‘Holy Well’ dedicated to St John the Baptist. The name ‘Killone’ itself comes from the Irish ‘Cil Eoin’, meaning ‘Church of John’.

A Walk Through Time

The abbey’s history is tied to the O’Brien family, one of Ireland’s most influential dynasties. Its first abbess, Slaney, was Donal Mór O’Brien’s daughter, and her death was recorded in 1260. She’s thought to have been the sister of Donnchadh Carbreach O’Brien, King of Thomond, who also established the Ennis Franciscan Friary not far away. Later abbesses included Dubhchollaithigh O’Brien, who passed away in 1350, and Regnalda O’Brien, whose death was noted in 1510.

One of the most colourful figures connected to Killone is Lady Honora O’Brien, its last recorded abbess. After taking religious vows in her youth, she scandalously eloped with Sir Roger O’Shaughnessy. They had children together, and the Pope eventually granted them dispensation to marry. After the dissolution of monasteries, the abbey was handed over to Honora’s father, Murrough O’Brien, 1st Earl of Thomond.

What to Look Out For

Killone Abbey’s layout centres around a cloister, with the church forming the northern side. Although much of it is now in ruins, the eastern range is still in decent shape thanks to conservation efforts. Don’t miss the beautiful Late Romanesque-style east window in the church, which even has a stairway built into the wall that leads to the roof. At the southeast corner of the church, a curious carving of a woman’s head and arms appears to hold the building aloft.

Explore the Area

A short walk from the abbey is St John’s Holy Well, which retains its spiritual significance today. Early maps show a ‘Pilgrim’s Path’ connecting Killone to Clare Abbey, suggesting it once lay on a well-travelled pilgrimage route.

Killone Abbey is one of several monastic sites in County Clare, alongside Clare Abbey, Ennis Friary, and Quin Friary. As part of the Newhall Estate, seat of the Lords of Kinfauns, it offers a rare combination of history and quiet beauty, and well rewards a visit.
